## (a) JEPA fundamentals: what it is and how it works

### Takeaway
JEPA predicts the representation (embedding) of a hidden or future part of the input from a visible context, rather than predicting pixels or tokens, and relies on a regularization or architectural trick to stop the embeddings collapsing to a constant. LeCun's 2022 position paper embeds JEPA in a modular agent (perception, world model, cost, actor, memory, configurator) that plans by minimizing cost over predicted latent futures; the 2025-26 work (LeJEPA/SIGReg, LeWM, H-JEPA) is largely an effort to make that recipe stable, simple, and hierarchical.

### Cited Findings
- [official/primary, via secondary summary] LeCun's "A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence" appeared on OpenReview on 27 June 2022. Summaries describe a modular design: perception module, a world model that fills in missing information (e.g., occluded objects) and predicts plausible future states, a cost module (hard-wired intrinsic cost plus a trainable critic that predicts future intrinsic cost, measured as "energy"), and a configurator acting as executive control that LeCun himself says remains under-specified. Open questions he acknowledges include how to configure the models to learn optimal latent features and how to design short-term memory. — [OpenReview PDF as cited by a course deck](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf); summaries at [JEPA wiki](https://mishig-jepawiki.hf.space/wiki/papers/lecun-position-paper) and [Shaped](https://www.shaped.ai/blog/yann-lecun-a-path-towards-autonomous-machine-intelligence), [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/yann-lecuns-vision-for-creating-autonomous-machines)
- [reported] Generic JEPA definition used in 2026 literature: encode context and target observations into a representation space and predict the target representation from the context representation, instead of reconstructing raw input; existing JEPA methods are "highly prone to collapse", motivating stabilization work. — [search-surfaced explainer/wiki](https://mishig-jepawiki.hf.space/wiki/concepts/latent-prediction); LeWM paper at [arXiv 2603.19312](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312)
- [official/primary, via secondary summary] LeJEPA (Balestriero and LeCun, arXiv 2511.08544, first submitted 11 Nov 2025): argues an isotropic Gaussian is the optimal distribution for JEPA embeddings to minimize downstream prediction risk, and introduces SIGReg (Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization), which uses random 1-D projections and characteristic-function matching, with cost linear in embedding dimension and sample size. A third-party summary says this removes stop-gradients, teacher-student/EMA networks and other collapse heuristics (check against the paper). Code: github.com/rbalestr-lab/lejepa. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08544), [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.08544v1), [code](https://github.com/rbalestr-lab/lejepa), [Turing Post](https://www.turingpost.com/p/lejepa)
- [official/primary, via secondary summary] LeWorldModel (LeWM; Maes, Le Lidec, Scieur, LeCun, Balestriero; Mila/Univ. de Montreal, NYU, Samsung SAIL, Brown; arXiv 2603.19312, v2 24 Mar 2026): first claimed JEPA trained stably end-to-end from raw pixels using only a next-embedding prediction loss plus a Gaussian-latent regularizer (SIGReg); reduces tunable loss hyperparameters from six to one versus the only other end-to-end alternative; about 15M parameters, trains on a single GPU in hours; plans up to 48x faster than foundation-model-based world models while competitive on 2D and 3D control tasks; latent space reportedly encodes physical structure and a "surprise" evaluation flags physically implausible events. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.19312v2), [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312), [hyper.ai mirror](https://hyper.ai/en/papers/2603.19312)
- [reported] A third-party explainer describes LeWM's architecture as ViT-Tiny encoder + 6-layer action-conditioned transformer predictor + SIGReg using an Epps-Pulley test, with lambda=0.1 the single hyperparameter (not checked against the paper). — [HF Space explainer](https://ar27111994-lewm-explainable.hf.space/)
- [official/primary, via secondary summary] V-JEPA 2-AC planning mechanism (the "world model + MPC" part of LeCun's agenda): with the video encoder frozen, an action-conditioned predictor forecasts the next latent state given current state and action; the planner scores candidate action sequences by L1 distance between predicted future representation and the goal-image representation, searches with the Cross-Entropy Method, executes only the first action, then re-plans (receding-horizon MPC). — [arXiv 2506.09985](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09985), [PureAI summary](https://pureai.com/Articles/2025/06/18/Meta-VJEPA-2.aspx)
- [official/primary, via secondary summary] Hierarchical JEPA realized in 2026: H-JEPA (arXiv 2610.06805, authors Zhang, Terver, Rabbat, LeCun, Balestriero; AMI Labs, NYU, INRIA Paris, Brown) stacks JEPA levels, each predicting further ahead in its own latent space; planning runs top-down with higher-level predicted states serving as subgoals for lower levels; higher levels drop fast-changing detail and keep slower state. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06805), [project site](https://h-jepa.com/), [ArXivSignals](https://arxivsignals.io/papers/2610.06805)
- [background knowledge, not re-verified] Earlier building blocks: I-JEPA (image JEPA with context encoder, EMA target encoder and predictor, masked-block latent prediction; CVPR 2023) [arXiv 2301.08243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08243); V-JEPA (video, Feb 2024) [arXiv 2404.08471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08471); VICReg (variance-invariance-covariance regularization for collapse prevention) [arXiv 2105.04906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.04906). The energy-based framing (low energy = compatible x,y pair; JEPA energy = prediction error in latent space) is from LeCun's 2022 paper and his energy-based-learning writings.
- [reported] LeCun is widely paraphrased as saying JEPA "is not generative AI", and analysts summarize his argument as: pixel-level prediction wastes capacity on unpredictable detail (leading to blurry outputs), so predict abstract representations instead. I did not locate a primary transcript of the "not generative AI" line; the "wastes capacity" phrasing is an analyst's reading. — [Quantum Zeitgeist guide](https://quantumzeitgeist.com/world-models-lecun-post-llm/), [Apolo history](https://www.apolo.us/blog-posts/yann-lecuns-jepa-and-world-models-a-thirty-years-history), [EntropyTown](https://entropytown.com/articles/2025-11-13-world-model-lecun-feifei-li/)

### Inferences
- [academic-inference] The arc LeJEPA (Nov 2025) -> LeWM (Mar 2026) -> H-JEPA (Oct 2026) shows the JEPA line moving from "needs EMA/stop-gradient/VICReg-style tricks" to "single Gaussian regularizer, one GPU, end-to-end from pixels," then to the long-promised hierarchical planner from the 2022 paper. The 2022 "H-JEPA" idea now has a first public, open implementation, though only on simulated mazes/Push-T and offline DROID clips.
- [academic-inference] In JEPA, "predict in latent space" does not mean "no pixels ever": LeWM still starts from raw pixels; the encoder simply learns what to keep. This matters for the 3D bridge, because anything geometric (depth, occupancy) must be implicitly retained by the encoder rather than explicitly represented.

### Gaps
- I could not open the 2022 position paper, I-JEPA, V-JEPA or VICReg papers; the collapse-prevention comparison (EMA vs VICReg vs SIGReg) rests on summaries and background knowledge.
- No primary transcript found for LeCun's "JEPA is not generative AI" wording.
- Conflicting detail: one summary of H-JEPA says higher-level baselines (three-level "HWM") score 38.7% on Visual AntMaze, while headlines compare only against flat LeWM at 18%.

## (c) State of JEPA models through Oct 2026 (I-JEPA, V-JEPA, V-JEPA 2 / 2-AC, V-JEPA 2.1, VL-JEPA, LeJEPA, LeWM, H-JEPA, and others)

### Takeaway
V-JEPA 2 (June 2025) is the flagship: pretrained on over 1M hours of internet video, post-trained on under 62 hours of Droid robot data, deployed zero-shot on Franka arms via latent-space MPC. In 2026 the line produced V-JEPA 2.1 (Mar 2026, dense features incl. depth), VL-JEPA (ICLR 2026), LeWM (Mar 2026) and H-JEPA (Oct 2026); I found no "V-JEPA 3".

### Cited Findings
- [official/primary, via secondary summary] V-JEPA 2: arXiv 2506.09985 (Assran, Bardes et al., Meta, June 2025), "Self-Supervised Video Models Enable Understanding, Prediction and Planning". Pretrained without actions on a dataset of over 1 million hours of internet video plus images; then V-JEPA 2-AC post-trained on under 62 hours of Droid robot-arm video paired with actions; zero-shot deployment on Franka arms in two different labs for pick-and-place with image goals, with no data from those robots and no task-specific training or reward. Reported limitations: sensitivity to camera position and long-horizon planning. Checkpoints on Hugging Face; code at github.com/facebookresearch/vjepa2. — [arXiv PDF](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09985), [PureAI](https://pureai.com/Articles/2025/06/18/Meta-VJEPA-2.aspx), [NYU RITS summary](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/introducing-v-jepa-2-metas-self-supervised-video-world-model-for-understanding-prediction-and-planning/), [LearnOpenCV](https://learnopencv.com/v-jepa-2-meta-world-model-robotics-guide/), [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2506.09985)
- [official/primary, via secondary summary] V-JEPA 2 benchmark headlines: 77.3 top-1 on Something-Something v2 and 39.7 recall@5 on Epic-Kitchens-100 action anticipation (state of the art at release). ## (d) 3D-flavored JEPA work and academic combinations with 3DGS / NeRF

### Takeaway
JEPA has been applied to point clouds (Point-JEPA, 3D-JEPA) and, as of Aug 2026, directly to Gaussian splats (Gaussian-JEPA, object-level, ShapeNet), but none of this comes from LeCun's, Meta's or AMI's teams. I found no JEPA + NeRF paper. The closest "latent world model over Gaussians" work (GWM) uses latent diffusion, not JEPA.

### Cited Findings
- [official/primary, via secondary summary] Gaussian-JEPA: "Joint-Embedding Predictive Learning for 3D Gaussian Splats" (arXiv 2608.15651, posted 16 Aug 2026, cs.CV; Bin Ren, Qi Ma, Yue Li, Zongyan Han, Yidi Li, Yuqian Fu, Rao Muhammad Anwer, Theo Gevers, Fahad Shahbaz Khan, Salman Khan). Predicts representations of held-out Gaussian token blocks from visible context using an online encoder plus a shared EMA target encoder at multiple scales, instead of reconstructing masked Gaussian attributes. Compared with matched Gaussian-MAE pretraining the authors report more consistent features across resampled inputs, more instance information under partial observation, and stronger frozen features for Gaussian completion; also transfers to part segmentation and object classification. Data: ShapeNet Gaussian assets from Gaussian-MAE (51,934 train, 520 test objects). Object-level, not scene-level. Numeric results not retrieved. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15651), [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15651), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.15651), [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/papers/gaussian-jepa-joint-embedding-predictive-learning-for-3d-gaussian-splats)
- [official/primary, via secondary summary] Precursors: ShapeSplat / Gaussian-MAE (arXiv 2408.10906) built a large Gaussian-splat dataset and masked-autoencoder pretraining (reported +0.55% accuracy and +0.4% mIoU over its point-cloud counterpart); Gaussian2Scene (arXiv 2506.08777) uses 3DGS as a bridge for scene-level point-cloud backbone pretraining with reconstruction-style (not JEPA) objectives. — [ShapeSplat (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2408.10906), [Gaussian2Scene](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08777)
- [official/primary, via secondary summary] Point-JEPA (Saito, Kudeshia, Poovvancheri; WACV 2025; arXiv 2404.16432): a "sequencer" orders point-cloud patch tokens so spatial neighbours are adjacent, making context/target selection cheap; reports 93.7% on ModelNet40 (linear evaluation per a secondary description) with 7.5 h pretraining. 3D-JEPA (arXiv 2409.15803): multi-block sampling, predicts target-block embeddings from a single context block without augmentations. Both position JEPA against Point-MAE-style reconstruction. — [Point-JEPA arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.16432), [CVF page](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2025/html/Saito_Point-JEPA_A_Joint_Embedding_Predictive_Architecture_for_Self-Supervised_Learning_on_WACV_2025_paper.html), [3D-JEPA arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.15803)
- [official/primary, via secondary summary] GWM: "Towards Scalable Gaussian World Models for Robotic Manipulation" (arXiv 2508.17600): a 3D variational encoder compresses Gaussians from a feed-forward reconstruction model into a latent space and a latent diffusion transformer predicts future Gaussians conditioned on robot actions; used as self-supervised representation learner for imitation learning and as a neural simulator for model-based RL. Secondary summaries report real Franka FR3 pick-and-place Diffusion Policy success rising from 35% to 65% (13/20) and a RoboCasa ablation from 4% (image world model) to 18% (Gaussians) to 24% (with 3D VAE) - not paper-verified. A related "MRO-GWM" (multi-rigid-object Gaussian world model; alphaXiv lists arXiv 2606.01950 under a different title, so the ID/title pairing is uncertain). — [GWM arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.17600), [GWM summary](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/gwm-towards-scalable-gaussian-world-models-for-robotic-manipulation), [alphaXiv 2606.01950](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2606.01950)
- [reported] Searches for "JEPA NeRF / neural radiance field" returned no paper combining the two; closest hits were LeWM (control-oriented) and the point-cloud JEPAs. — [search-surfaced LeWM page](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312) (negative result; see Gaps)
- [official/primary, via secondary summary] LeWM evaluates on "2D and 3D control tasks" - this refers to 3D control environments, not 3D reconstruction/scene representations. — [arXiv 2603.19312](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.19312v2)

### Inferences
- [academic-inference] Gaussian-JEPA's motivation (latent targets are "sturdier" than reconstructing one sampled version of the scene) is the standard JEPA-vs-MAE argument applied to splats; it shows splats can be treated as a tokenizable input modality for JEPA-style SSL, which is the most concrete technical bridge in the literature. It does not show that JEPA world models can plan over splats.
- [academic-inference] GWM is the nearest "latent world model over Gaussians" and is generative (diffusion), i.e., it sits on the Li-side of the generative/predictive divide even though it predicts in a learned latent space; this is a reminder that "latent" does not equal "JEPA".

- Gaussian-JEPA numbers and author affiliations not retrieved; no AMI/Meta/NYU co-authors seen in its author list.
- No scene-level JEPA over Gaussians or over Marble-style generated worlds found.

## (f) Conceptual bridge: how Marble-style explicit 3D (splats) could serve JEPA-style systems, and what supports or contradicts it

### Takeaway
There is real published support for "splats as a simulator substrate for robot learning" (RoboGSim, GaussGym, SplatSim, Real-to-Sim evaluation, NVIDIA's Marble-to-Isaac Sim tutorial) and for "latent world models trained/evaluated in such environments" in principle, but I found no published work that trains or evaluates a JEPA/V-JEPA model on Marble-generated worlds, and no statement from either company proposing it. The bridge is an engineering inference.

### Cited Findings
- [official/primary, vendor] NVIDIA's developer blog documents a Marble to Isaac Sim pipeline: export Gaussian splats (PLY) and a collider mesh (GLB) from Marble, convert to USD with Omniverse NuRec, import into Isaac Sim, add a robot. Custom-scene downloads require a paid World Labs subscription; sample assets are free. — [NVIDIA Developer Blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/simulate-robotic-environments-faster-with-nvidia-isaac-sim-and-world-labs-marble), [Radiance Fields coverage](https://radiancefields.com/nvidia-publishes-blog-on-marble-workflow-for-robotics-simulation-with-isaac-sim) (publication date not captured)

### Inferences
- [academic-inference] Plausible bridge roles, ordered by evidence strength:
  1. Simulation environments/evaluation substrate: strongest support. Marble (splats + collider GLB) -> Isaac Sim via NuRec is documented by NVIDIA; GaussGym/RoboGSim show splat renders can train/evaluate pixel-input policies. A JEPA world model (LeWM/DINO-WM/V-JEPA-2-AC style) could be trained or evaluated on rollouts rendered in such scenes. Untested for JEPA specifically.
  2. Training-data source for video JEPAs: weaker. Rendered splat tours give clean multi-view, camera-controlled video with exact poses and depth, which could supply view-consistency/3D supervision signals or augment pretraining; but V-JEPA 2 was pretrained on 1M+ hours of real video, and synthetic splat renders have visual and physics artifacts (static scenes, baked lighting), so domain gap is a risk.
  3. Structured scene prior: speculative. Splat tokens (Gaussian-JEPA) show splats can be an SSL input modality; a JEPA that consumes splats as context could predict occluded geometry in latent space, but no one has shown it at scene level.
- [academic-inference] Contradicting or limiting evidence: (i) Marble scenes are static assets; JEPA world models and MPC need action-conditioned dynamics (contact, objects moving), which splats do not supply - Marble's collider mesh gives collision but not articulated or deformable physics; (ii) RoboGSim's manual alignment and lack of reported alignment error show real2sim pipelines still need careful engineering; (iii) GWM, the closest Gaussian latent world model, is generative/diffusion-based, not JEPA, so "latent" Gaussian world models exist but on the other side of the LeCun critique; (iv) Marble's architecture is unpublished, so claims about its geometric consistency or metric accuracy cannot be assessed.
- [speculative] An AMI-Labs-style system could use Marble-like splat scenes only as an offline data generator while keeping its own representation learned and non-generative at inference time, which would be compatible with LeCun's stance (he objects to generating pixels as the model's mechanism, not necessarily to using rendered data). No source shows LeCun endorsing this.
- [speculative] AMD's acquisition of World Labs could push Marble toward AMD-hardware robotics/simulation workflows; irrelevant to JEPA unless AMI builds on AMD hardware, for which there is no evidence.

### Gaps
- No paper found training/evaluating V-JEPA, LeWM, DINO-WM or H-JEPA on Marble-generated or any Gaussian-splat simulator scenes.
- NVIDIA blog date, Marble export spec from official World Labs docs, and sim-to-real success figures for GaussGym/SplatSim were not retrieved.
- No independent head-to-head benchmark of Genie 3 vs Cosmos vs Isaac Sim for robot-learning data quality.

## (g) Comparing representations: explicit (mesh, splats, NeRF), implicit neural fields, learned latent world states (JEPA), generative video world models (Genie 3, Cosmos)

### Takeaway
The evidence base for a rigorous comparison is thin; what exists is qualitative. Explicit 3D is controllable, inspectable and physics-ready but static unless coupled to a simulator; learned latent states (JEPA) are cheap and plan-friendly but opaque and unproven at scale; generative video models offer diversity and visuals but weaker controllability, physics guarantees and high compute.

### Cited Findings
- [reported] Explicit/parametric simulation (Isaac Sim): strengths are physics and sensor fidelity, parametric control over geometry/materials/boundary conditions; weakness is dependence on manually built or imported environments. — [Metavert comparison](https://metavert.io/compare/world-models-vs-simulating-reality)
- [reported] Generative world models (Genie 3): prompt-driven worlds; editing via prompts or latent manipulation rather than parametric control; may lack the physical accuracy of explicit simulators. Cosmos: generates synthetic training data for robotics/AV; often combined with Isaac Sim/Genesis pipelines. — [Metavert comparison](https://metavert.io/compare/world-models-vs-simulating-reality), [Spheron on Genesis + Cosmos](https://www.spheron.network/blog/deploy-genesis-physics-engine-gpu-cloud-robot-simulation/)
- [official/primary, via secondary summary] Splat/explicit scene properties relevant to simulators: depth is a by-product of rasterization and simulation speed can exceed 100k steps/s (GaussGym); Marble exports a collider mesh in addition to splats for physics. — [GaussGym](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.15352), [NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/simulate-robotic-environments-faster-with-nvidia-isaac-sim-and-world-labs-marble)
- [official/primary, via secondary summary] Learned latent world states for planning: V-JEPA 2-AC plans with CEM-MPC in latent space; LeWM plans up to 48x faster than foundation-model-based world models with a 15M-parameter model; DINO-WM plans over frozen DINOv2 features. — [V-JEPA 2](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09985), [LeWM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.19312v2), [DINO-WM](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/zhou25t.html)
- [reported] Genie 3 generates one frame at a time while attending to previously generated frames (autoregressive consistency via memory); Marble persists downloadable 3D assets rather than streaming frames. — [Medium "Words to Worlds"](https://medium.com/@hiconcep/words-to-worlds-the-last-engine-733c4162d85a), [Eka Ventures](https://ekavc.substack.com/p/world-models-impact-edition)
- [reported] Planning framing from analysts: the split is whether a world model should be something you look at (renderable) or something an agent plans with (latent). — [Eka Ventures newsletter](https://ekavc.substack.com/p/world-models-impact-edition), [EntropyTown](https://entropytown.com/articles/2025-11-13-world-model-lecun-feifei-li/), [Themesis "Five competing approaches"](https://themesis.com/2026/01/07/world-models-five-competing-approaches/)
- [background knowledge, not re-verified] NeRF (implicit radiance field, 2020) vs 3D Gaussian Splatting (explicit anisotropic Gaussians, 2023): NeRF is slow to render and edit; 3DGS renders in real time and is explicitly editable, which is why simulators adopted splats. Canonical sources: [NeRF arXiv 2003.08934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08934), [3DGS arXiv 2308.04079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04079).

### Inferences
- [academic-inference] Rough comparison (qualitative, my synthesis; not benchmarked in any cited source):
  - Meshes/CAD (explicit): highest controllability and physics-readiness; costly to author; static unless rigged; best as simulator ground truth.
  - 3D Gaussian splats (explicit, appearance-oriented): photoreal, real-time, editable, depth for free; weak native physics (needs collider/proxy mesh); static scenes; good as render layer in sim (GaussGym, RoboGSim, Marble->Isaac).
  - NeRF / implicit neural fields: compact and continuous, slower to render, harder to edit or physically simulate; largely superseded by splats in recent robot-simulation work cited here (no JEPA+NeRF work found).
  - JEPA latent states: smallest compute for planning (15M-param LeWM, CEM over latents), no rendering, learned from observation; opaque, weaker interpretability, geometry only implicitly encoded; planning demonstrated mostly in simulation and narrow tabletop settings.
  - Generative video world models (Genie 3, Cosmos): richest diversity and zero authoring cost; compute-heavy; controllability and physical correctness are the weak points; useful as data engines or interfaces rather than as planners.
- [academic-inference] These are complementary layers rather than rivals: explicit 3D for scene construction and ground-truth physics, generative models for scene diversity, latent JEPA models for fast prediction and planning. This complementarity is my reading of the cited sources, not a claim made by either lab.

### Gaps
- No quantitative cross-representation benchmark found (compute, physical accuracy, planning success) for splats vs NeRF vs JEPA vs Genie 3/Cosmos.
- Cosmos version names conflict across sources (Predict2.5/Transfer2.5/Reason2 vs "Cosmos 3"); Genie 3 details come from secondary summaries.
- Low-reliability vendor/aggregator comparison pages (e.g., tech-insider.org "Genie 3 vs Marble vs NVIDIA Cosmos") were not used for claims.
