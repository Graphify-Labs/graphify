The short answer

Part 1

World Labs and Marble

World Labs, co-founded by Fei-Fei Li, Justin Johnson, Christoph Lassner and Ben Mildenhall (Maginative), sells Marble, a multimodal model that turns text, images, video, panoramas or coarse 3D layouts into persistent, downloadable 3D worlds delivered as Gaussian splats plus collider meshes (World Labs). Its architecture is undisclosed and no Marble paper was found; the World API (21 Jan 2026) is asynchronous and polling-only, the one announced next step is the Atlas "omni" model (1 Sep 2026), and AMD's roughly $8.2B all-stock acquisition (announced 28 Sep 2026) is widely reported but still pending (AMD Newsroom).

Part 2

Blender as hub

The bridge works today but is lopsided: Blender 5.3 (stable release scheduled 10 Nov 2026) imports PLY, SPZ and USD splats natively but cannot export them (Cinevva), so the return leg runs through add-ons that write INRIA PLY, then PlayCanvas splat-transform to ratified GLB with KHR_gaussian_splatting, SOG or SPZ (KHR README), and the user's x3d-blender-io fork for the X3D 4.1 GaussianSplats node that X_ITE renders (X_ITE docs); Marble's export axis convention is unresolved in the sources and needs a test import.

Part 3

JEPA

No organizational link between Yann LeCun's AMI Labs and World Labs was found (NVIDIA invests in both); the relationship is conceptual, a generative explicit-3D world model versus a predictive latent one, and the adjacent evidence (NVIDIA's Marble-to-Isaac Sim pipeline, splat-based robot simulators, Gaussian-JEPA on object-level splats, V-JEPA 2.1's depth probe) never joins the two: no one has trained or evaluated a JEPA on Marble worlds (NVIDIA; Gaussian-JEPA).

Part 4

AI-enabled X3D

The parts exist (x3d_mcp's four-layer validation, X_ITE's splat node, Blender MCP, simulator-in-the-loop critics) but no published system combines agents, a scene graph, splats and JEPA (x3d_mcp); the closing PROPOSAL specifies role-scoped agents behind a validated, signed, transactional edit layer, with a JEPA-style critic as a gated research stage rather than a dependency.

Part 1 · The model

Marble discloses its outputs but not its architecture

World Labs has documented Marble's interface in detail and its internals not at all. Inputs are text, a single image, multiple images (each with an optional azimuth), video, a panorama or a coarse 3D layout (World Labs blog; API quickstart) S. Chisel, flagged experimental, separates structure from style: the user blocks out boxes, planes, walls or imported GLB/FBX assets and a text prompt sets the look (Chisel basics) S. Outputs are splats as SPZ ("Marble's native splat format") or PLY at about 2M splats full-resolution or about 500k, a 2560 x 1280 equirectangular panorama, a coarse collider GLB, and a high-quality GLB that the docs say is derived from the splat, so reconstruction artifacts are expected (Export specs; Mesh export) S. The model line is marble-1.0-draft, marble-1.0, marble-1.1 and marble-1.1-plus; Plus grows a world by "dynamic cubes", described by press as up to five extra spatial units at 300 credits each (Radiance Fields) T. Region-selective Expand and a Composer that assembles several worlds into one space come from an announcement summary (visual-slam-roadmap) T.

What is missing is the substance of a technical report. No Marble paper, benchmark, model code or weights were located, and World Labs' GitHub organization holds an API client, examples, agent skills, atlas-camera-studio and marble-multiview-inpainting but no research code (github.com/worldlabsai) D. The question of whether the network emits splat parameters directly is open: a third-party write-up of the Latent Space interview paraphrases Johnson as saying Marble natively outputs Gaussian splats, while another summary has him arguing splats are only the current atomic unit (Riffon; Latent Space) T. A reported September 2026 podcast has Johnson saying Marble bottlenecked every output through splats and that Atlas makes new-view prediction the core primitive, generating splats only when needed, but that is a single secondary source (My Living AI) T.

Inference The disclosed artifacts point toward "generate consistent views plus depth, then lift to splats". Panorama is a first-class export, and the Atlas-era APIs expose posed RGB-D primitives: images2PosedRGBD returns an EXR depth map and camera poses, atlasGenerate returns camera-conditioned frames, and the inpainting tool takes 2 to 16 posed RGB-D views plus depth as EXR, NPY or NPZ (atlas-camera-studio; marble-multiview-inpainting) D. Fixed output tiers of roughly 2M, 500k and 100k splats and a flat 1,500-credit price suggest bounded, fixed-cost inference rather than per-scene optimization. The nearest published families are CAT3D (multi-view diffusion then reconstruction, with Mildenhall as co-author), Lyra (a video-diffusion latent decoded to 3DGS), Bolt3D and feed-forward lifters such as VGGT and GS-LRM (CAT3D; Lyra; VGGT) MS. An independent critique of Atlas launch material argues depth maps and images are turned into splats after generation (Bret Kerr) T, but it concerns Atlas, not Marble. Speculation The most parsimonious pipeline is prompt, then panorama or multi-view diffusion with depth, then a feed-forward or short-optimization splat lift, then LoD downsampling and mesh baking; the "Marble 5 API" wording in the atlas-camera-studio README hints that the Atlas backbone already sits behind Marble-branded endpoints. Neither has direct evidence.

The representation itself is well understood M. NeRF (Mildenhall et al., 2020) stores a scene as an MLP mapping position and direction to density and view-dependent colour, rendered by volumetric ray marching and trained per scene (arXiv 2003.08934); Instant-NGP's hash grid cut training to seconds or minutes (arXiv 2201.05989). 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) replaced the implicit field with millions of explicit anisotropic Gaussians, each carrying a position, a scale-and-quaternion covariance, an opacity and spherical-harmonic colour (about 59 floats at SH degree 3), rasterized through depth-sorted tiles at real-time rates (arXiv 2308.04079). Inference explicit primitives are why splats are editable, streamable and LoD-able where NeRFs are not, and why a generator that must hand assets to engines would pick them. The cost is baked lighting, no guaranteed surface (hence "derived" meshes), floaters and aliasing.

RTFM and Atlas are different bets from Marble. RTFM is an autoregressive diffusion transformer trained end-to-end on video that builds no explicit 3D representation; its world is the KV-cache of pose-tagged frames, with "context juggling" retrieving spatially nearby frames (RTFM blog; Radiance Fields) S. Inference Marble's persistence is asset-level (the splat file is the world state, so it renders identically from any pose), whereas RTFM's is context-level and can drift after long excursions. Atlas is reported as a multimodal autoregressive diffusion transformer (rectified flow) that takes camera geometry as an input and returns up to about a minute of 1440p video or point clouds and splats, with World Labs claiming it beats specialized models and independent commentary questioning benchmark fairness (Kingy AI; Winzheng) T; World Labs says Atlas "will power future versions of Marble" (Radiance Fields) T.

Spark is the best-documented piece because it is open source D. It renders all splats in one instanced draw call after a background-worker bucket sort whose order "lags the render by at least one frame", merges with mesh geometry through the Z-buffer, and supports per-splat transforms, skinning and procedural splats (system-design). Version 2.0 added a hierarchical LoD tree, the streamable .RAD format fetched by HTTP range requests, and a shared GPU page table (default 16M splats) intended for scenes of 100M or more splats; World Labs' own splat budgets are about 1M for Quest 3, 1 to 3M for phones and 1 to 5M for computers (new-features-2.0; performance). The compact PackedSplats encoding stores centres as float16, which loses precision away from the origin; the 32-byte ExtSplats added in 2.0 uses float32 centres (packed-splats). Inference a full-resolution Marble world (about 2M splats) fits the desktop budget but not Quest or phones without the 500k tier or LoD.

Marble's limits are the limits of a generated, splat-based asset. Worlds are plausible rather than metric: hidden regions are invented, and a World Labs essay warns that generated geometry can look right while having self-intersections or wrong scale that produce nonsensical physics (taxonomy essay, via search summary) S. The docs concede "uneven geometry, holes, and blobby or 'floater' surfaces" in derived meshes and call colliders simplified (Mesh export docs) S; an independent hands-on test found lower quality away from the input viewpoint, poor overlap when splats and conventional meshes share a scene, odd collision behaviour and weak early mesh quality (design4real) T. Worlds are static (dynamics appear only in post-processed export videos), splats carry no semantic, instance or material labels, lighting is baked into view-dependent colour, and no quantitative accuracy study exists (Latent Space; SPARK_V2.md) T. Inference treat Marble as a generator of visual scenes with coarse proxies, not of simulation-ready worlds; physics, semantics and metric scale must be added downstream.

Part 1 · The API

The World API is an asynchronous REST service with a thin public roadmap

The World API launched on 21 Jan 2026 as a REST service at https://api.worldlabs.ai/marble/v1/..., authenticated with a WLT-Api-Key header (Generate reference) S; the official Python client README confirms the header (worldlabs-api-python) D. The documented flow is: get a key and buy credits at platform.worldlabs.ai, POST worlds:generate, poll the returned operation until it is done, then GET the world (Quickstart) S. The request body takes display_name, model, permission, seed, tags and a world_prompt discriminated by type: text (with a disable_recaption flag), image (with is_pano defaulting to auto, so a valid equirectangular image skips the panorama-generation step), multi-image (an array of images with optional azimuth) and video. Generation usually takes about five minutes; polling is free; and errors are 400 (invalid request, policy violation or bad model), 402 (insufficient credits), 429 (rate limit) and 500 (Rate limits) S. Machine-readable indexes live at /llms.txt and /llms-full.txt (llms-full.txt) S. No official webhook was found; every excerpt uses polling, although one press summary mentions a callback (blockchain.news) T.

Money and rules are the constraints that shape an agent loop. API credits cost $1.00 per 1,250 ($5 minimum), never expire and cannot be bought through the Marble app; a standard world costs 1,500 credits, a text prompt adds an 80-credit panorama step, draft costs 150, and Plus adds up to 1,500 more (API pricing) S. Inference at the published rate a standard world is about $1.20, a draft about $0.12 and a worst-case text-prompted Plus world 3,080 credits, about $2.46. Default limits are about three starts a minute and 60 an hour, with approved accounts reaching about 30 standard and 90 draft starts a minute; a 429 means the start was not accepted (Rate limits) S. The Terms of Service exist in two versions with different numbering and, as paraphrased by secondary sources, give paid users ownership of outputs with World Labs attribution on reasonable request and limit free accounts to personal non-commercial use; one unverified analysis adds a licence to use user content for model training, with paid users able to opt out only going forward (Terms) T. The ToS text was not read, so written confirmation from World Labs is the prudent step before shipping a product.

Official tooling is thin but agent-friendly. worldlabs-api-python (MIT, Python 3.12+) offers sync and async clients, loads SPZ into a Gaussian3D object and renders with CUDA gsplat; worldlabs-api-examples has Node.js and Python scripts plus a depth-PNG Chisel example; and worldlabsai/skills (Apache-2.0) packages two skills for Codex and Claude Code, worldlabs (image generation and editing, camera and depth estimation, task and asset management) and atlas-blender (Blender scene work, requiring Blender 5.1+ and an Atlas add-on) (python; examples; skills) D. Community builds include FastMCP and Node MCP servers for the Marble API, a Unity 6 editor client that applies coordinate correction and a spatial-QA tool that handles semantics_metadata (worldlabs-mcp; Marble-WorldLabs-MCP-Server; Unity client; worldspec) T. No official JS/TS SDK, Unity, Unreal, Omniverse or Blender add-on for Marble worlds was found; the one World Labs-authored Blender integration targets Atlas.

What comes next is signalled, not published. A secondary summary of the release notes records "no published roadmap" (release notes) T. The signals are Atlas, which is in early access with a waitlisted beta API whose sample-app outputs expire after seven days (atlas-camera-studio) D and which the Marble site says is "coming to Marble soon" (Marble enterprise page) S; Johnson's remark that creative control is a main theme going forward (TechCrunch) T; a scheduled switch of the default model, documented as marble-1.0 on one page and marble-1.1 on another, so model should always be set explicitly (Models) S; and the AMD close expected by end of 2026, where coverage notes neither announcement addresses Marble or API pricing, shutdown or migration (AI News) T. Inference the likeliest next step is Atlas capabilities (camera-conditioned video, depth, point-cloud and splat output) reaching the main API; no date exists. Not found: World Labs statements on real-time streaming, dynamic scenes, physics, USD or KHR_gaussian_splatting export, which is absence of evidence only because the blog index and X feed were not read in full.

Part 2 · Blender as hub

Blender can be the hub, but only the import leg is native

Blender is a credible transmission point because its input side has just become first-class and its output side is rich in add-ons, but the two halves come from different places and the seam between them is where errors hide. The user's own repository supplies the X3D half; the web, Unreal, Unity and Omniverse halves come from third-party tools and Khronos, PlayCanvas and NVIDIA formats.

Blender 5.3 imports splats but does not export them

Blender's main branch gained "implements importing gaussian splats from PLY" and "makes the Import PLY node handle gaussian splats" on 16 Sep 2026 and "sanitizes the SPZ and PLY importers against malformed files" on 17 Sep, with PLY robustness fixes still landing on 20 to 21 Oct (blender/blender commits) D. The release notes, seen only through summaries, say Workbench, EEVEE and Cycles render splats with an emissive default shader and a radiance attribute, relighting is "rudimentary", and three limits apply: performance is "not ideal", splats are assumed sRGB while Cycles and EEVEE work in linear, and Apply Transform does not yet handle the new scale, rotation and spherical-harmonics attributes (Blender 5.3 rendering notes) S. Secondary coverage adds that PLY and SPZ (up to v4, with automatic coordinate conversion) import, that splats live as a new "3D Gaussian Splat" Point Cloud type operated on by Geometry Nodes (new Import SPZ and Set Point Cloud Type nodes), that USD ParticleField splats import, and that there is no splat export (Digital Production; Cinevva) S. Stable release is scheduled for 10 Nov 2026, so Blender 5.3 is unreleased on 22 Oct and no release-candidate report was found. Blender's notes describe the splat convention as "Z as the Forward Axis, -Y as Up axis" and warn that SPZ files saved outside the SPZ convention may need manual rotation (Blender 5.3 rendering notes) S. No evidence was found of KHR_gaussian_splatting support in the KhronosGroup/glTF-Blender-IO add-on, so GLB splat files must be written outside Blender.

The add-on ecosystem fills the export gap unevenly. KIRI's 3DGS Render (repository page says GPL-2.0; 1.2k stars) imports 3DGS PLY, edits with Blender selections and modifiers, composites splats with Blender scenes, exports edited 3DGS and animated PLY sequences, rotates SH on bake in 5.1.0, and is "moving into community-led maintenance" (repo; releases) D. Gaussian Splat Patch (Blender 5.3+ extension, GPL-3.0, built against the 5.3 alpha) erases, clones, heals and merges splats while rotating SH bands 1 to 3, and exports standard INRIA PLY (repo) D. AssetKit's Blender extension imports SPZ, L-GSC and Gaussian PLY/glTF for preview only (release v0.3.0) D; blender-splat-render adds SH relighting but is early (repo) D; PLAN8VR's exporter writes synthetic splat PLY from mesh geometry (repo) D; and Gauss Cannon and BlenderNeRF export COLMAP or NeRF-style datasets for retraining (Gauss Cannon; BlenderNeRF) D. Paid options such as SplatForge and Fast Splatting rest on vendor claims (Superhive) V. No Blender splat-to-mesh add-on was verified; Houdini's GSOPs bake splats to points, meshes and density volumes (GSOPs) D. Inference the hub is an ingest, align and clean hub with a bring-your-own export, and a native exporter in 5.4 or later would be the largest single improvement (Speculation on timing).

The reverse leg, from engines and tools into Blender, is narrower. Blender's native importers take PLY, SPZ (to v4) and USD ParticleField, so splats trained or exported by 3DGRUT (USD, NuRec USDZ or PLY), LichtFeld Studio (PLY, SOG, SPZ) or SuperSplat arrive as PLY, SPZ or USD (3DGRUT; LichtFeld Studio; SuperSplat) D. No verified Blender importer exists for SOG, .rad or GLB with KHR_gaussian_splatting (AssetKit previews glTF splats only), so web-delivery files must be converted back to PLY first, and splat-transform cannot read GLB at all, which means the hub needs its own GLB reader or gsbox, which reads GLB from v4.7.1 (splat-transform; gsbox) D. Where transforms, units and handedness live. In Blender, keep rotation and scale on parent empties or object transforms and do not apply them, because Apply Transform skips the splat attributes (Blender 5.3 notes) S. In X3D, put the transform on a Transform above the Inline, as x3d-blender-io does when it carries Blender's Z-up as a Y-up rotation (README) D. In glTF, leave it on the node, since the extension rotates SH by the node rotation (KHR README) D. Bake only in a tool that rotates SH explicitly: 3DGS Render's bake and Patch's merge do, while splat-transform's documentation does not say (3DGS Render releases; Patch; splat-transform) D. For units, Blender works in metres, x3d-blender-io multiplies splat scales by the unit factor on import, and blender-splat-render needs Blender's unit scale set to the real scene size for correct depth of field (PR #12; repo) D. For handedness, Unity and Unreal are left-handed while Blender, glTF and three.js are right-handed, so a mirror, not just a rotation, is needed for the first two, and Babylon.js performs no conversion at all (Babylon doc) [D / general engine knowledge].

Part 3 · JEPA

No one has joined Marble and JEPA, but the pieces sit side by side

Verdict. The notes found no organizational, advisory, investor or collaborative link between Yann LeCun's AMI Labs and Fei-Fei Li's World Labs. Searches for a LeCun role at World Labs, a LeCun comment on Marble or Genie, and a Li comment on JEPA or AMI returned nothing; the one confirmed shared node is NVIDIA, an investor in both (Sifted; SiliconANGLE) T, which is a weak signal because NVIDIA invests widely. Public pairing of the two is third-party framing (EntropyTown; Hacker News) T. The negative is search-limited: the full texts of Li's essays, the taxonomy post and the Latent Space and a16z podcasts were not scanned for the words JEPA, LeCun or AMI, and posts on X, LinkedIn and Threads are not indexed by the search tool. AMI Labs raised about $1.03B at a $3.5B pre-money valuation (announced 9 to 10 Mar 2026) under CEO Alexandre LeBrun, with LeCun as executive chairman and a CEO statement of "at least a year of research before deploying our first real-world applications" (TechCrunch; Futurum) T. Inference AMD's purchase of World Labs pulls World Labs toward AMD's ecosystem while NVIDIA backs AMI, which weakens even the shared-investor story.

The conceptual divide is generative explicit 3D versus predictive latent state. A JEPA predicts the representation of a hidden or future part of the input from a visible context instead of reconstructing pixels or tokens, and needs a regularizer or architectural trick to stop embeddings collapsing; LeCun's 2022 position paper places it inside a modular agent (perception, world model, cost with a trainable critic, actor, memory, configurator) that plans by minimizing cost over predicted latent futures (OpenReview PDF; Shaped) S. Marble does the opposite on each axis that matters here: it generates an explicit, inspectable asset that a human or engine renders. Analysts frame the split as whether a world model is something you look at or something an agent plans with (Eka Ventures) T, and one critique argues Marble models only the part of the world people can see (EntropyTown) T. An academic roadmap places the JEPA family in the "latent-space simulator" category of world models (arXiv 2607.06401) S. Inference a JEPA-style model fits the planner and latent-simulator slots of World Labs' renderer, simulator and planner taxonomy, though World Labs never made that placement. Both camps reject the LLM-only path, and Li has argued that a robot unable to predict what happens when a cup falls off a table cannot work effectively in the real world (The Neuron) T, a prediction claim that LeCun would plausibly share (Inference). The JEPA line itself matured within a year: LeJEPA's SIGReg regularizer (Nov 2025), LeWorldModel (Mar 2026; about 15M parameters, one GPU, end-to-end from pixels) and H-JEPA (Oct 2026), a hierarchical model whose three-level version reached 73.3% success on Visual AntMaze against 18.0% for flat LeWM (LeJEPA; LeWM; H-JEPA) S.

NeRF and splats connect through lineage and simulators, not through JEPA. Mildenhall created NeRF and co-authored CAT3D and DreamFusion, and two searches found no paper combining JEPA with NeRF (NeRF; CAT3D) M. Inference splats have largely replaced NeRF in the robot-simulation work cited here because 3DGS renders in real time and edits per primitive (3DGS) M. JEPA has met 3D elsewhere. Point-JEPA and 3D-JEPA work on point clouds (Point-JEPA; 3D-JEPA) S. Gaussian-JEPA (arXiv 2608.15651, Aug 2026) predicts held-out Gaussian-token blocks in representation space on ShapeNet splat assets and reports more consistent features than a matched Gaussian-MAE; it is object-level, comes from neither LeCun's team nor Meta nor AMI, and its numbers were not retrieved (arXiv; Radiance Fields) S.

On depth, physics and dexterity the JEPA evidence is real but narrow. For depth, V-JEPA 2.1 (Mar 2026) reports 0.307 RMSE on NYUv2 monocular depth with a linear probe, the strongest evidence that a JEPA video encoder keeps metric-like 3D structure without 3D supervision, though a probe is not reconstruction or novel-view synthesis (BDTechTalks; Meta README) S. For physics, LeWM's latent reportedly encodes physical structure and a "surprise" evaluation flags physically implausible events (LeWM) S. For dexterity, V-JEPA 2-AC was post-trained on under 62 hours of Droid robot video after pretraining on over 1M hours of internet video, then drove Franka arms zero-shot in two labs on pick-and-place with image goals, planning with cross-entropy search over latent futures and re-planning after each action; reported limits are camera-position sensitivity and long-horizon planning (arXiv 2506.09985) S. That is gripper-level pick-and-place, not in-hand dexterity. No published JEPA result shows long-horizon real-world manipulation or navigation, the success rates attributed to V-JEPA 2-AC (about 80% for pick-and-place) come from one secondary source, and V-JEPA 2.1's reported "20-point" real-robot grasp gain is ambiguous between points and percent (Emergent Mind) T. On the Marble side, worlds are static with a coarse collider, depth falls out of splat rasterization, and the SceniX acquisition aims at real-to-sim-to-real policy training rather than latent prediction (GaussGym; World Labs) S.

What actually connects them is simulation. NVIDIA's developer blog documents Marble PLY splats plus a GLB collider converted to USD and imported into Isaac Sim (NVIDIA) S; RoboGSim and GaussGym use 3DGS as the renderer inside physics simulators, GaussGym at over 100,000 steps a second with depth from rasterization (RoboGSim; GaussGym) S; and the Gaussian World Model (GWM) compresses Gaussians into a latent space and predicts future Gaussians with a latent diffusion transformer, so it is a latent Gaussian world model on the generative side of the divide, a reminder that latent does not mean JEPA (GWM) S. Inference, ordered by strength of evidence: splat scenes as a simulation and evaluation substrate for a latent world model are best supported, and LeWM and DINO-WM are cheap enough to use that way; rendered splat tours as a data source with exact poses and depth are weaker because of static scenes, baked lighting and the gap to 1M hours of real video; splats as a structured input prior are least supported, since Gaussian-JEPA shows only that splats can be tokenized for self-supervised learning. Speculation an AMI-style system could use Marble-like scenes purely as an offline data generator while keeping its own representation non-generative at inference, which would fit LeCun's objection to generating pixels as the model's mechanism; no source shows him endorsing it.

The sandbox could not open worldlabs.ai, docs.worldlabs.ai, atlas-beta.worldlabs.ai, arxiv.org, khronos.org, web3d.org, blender.org, openusd.org and several news and trade sites, so every World Labs docs and blog claim is a search summary (S), every arXiv ID marked as from memory (M) was not re-fetched, and the planned second verification round never ran. The gaps that matter most are these. No Marble paper, architecture, training-data description or benchmark was found. Marble's SH degree, colour space, official axis convention and SPZ scale formula were not retrieved, and neither was an official World Labs Blender guide: a secondary source says one exists, but only the Unreal and Unity pages were confirmed. The Terms of Service, data-retention periods, the permission field's values, the list-worlds path, media upload endpoints and any webhook are unconfirmed. No evidence was found for per-node LLM agents in scene graphs, for usdchecker rules from a primary source, for a C2PA binding to 3D, for MPEG Gaussian splat coding status, for X3D 4.1 ISO publication, or for Web3D 2026 proceedings beyond the x3d_mcp paper. Blender's PR #163102 text, whether EEVEE and Cycles evaluate view-dependent SH at render time, and 5.3 release-candidate status were not seen. The AMD close and any post-8 Oct update were not found. Independent benchmarks of splat formats, engine plugins and Atlas are absent, so every performance figure here is a vendor figure.

Summary

Conclusion

The strongest result is a standards convergence, not a Marble-to-JEPA link. glTF's ratified splat extension, the X3D node that mirrors it field for field and OpenUSD's ParticleField schema all describe the same Gaussian data, so a hub that normalises conventions once at ingest and proves every spoke by round trip can be correct in a way no single engine plugin guarantees. Blender's job is ingest, alignment and cleaning with export left to add-ons, and the practical dangers are silent convention errors (axes, quaternion order, SH rotation, activation functions, Apply Transform) rather than missing features. That is why the recipe starts with a $0.12 asymmetric-marker test and ends with a bit-exact round trip, the discipline the user's own PR #12 already applied to X3D.

The JEPA question changes shape once the evidence sits side by side. Marble supplies static, visually rich worlds with coarse colliders; JEPA models learn dynamics from action-conditioned experience; the missing ingredient on both sides is dynamics, which only a physics simulator supplies. The first defensible role for a JEPA-style model here is therefore as a critic or planner over simulator rollouts in Marble-derived scenes, not as an editor of a scene graph, and nothing published tests even that. Whether such a critic beats a physics check plus a debiased VLM judge is an empirical question, so the proposal below turns it into a go/no-go gate. Platform risk (the AMD close, Atlas reshaping Marble's endpoints) argues for an adapter layer and local caching from the first day.

PROPOSAL: role-scoped agents inside a validated X3D scene graph, with a gated JEPA critic

This section is a design proposal, not a finding. Each element carries one label: Grounded (a cited source demonstrates the component or pattern), Inference (reasoned from cited evidence but not demonstrated) or Speculation (plausible, with no direct evidence). The survey behind it found no published system that combines agents, a scene graph, splats and JEPA; the nearest neighbours are x3d_mcp (validated X3D editing over MCP), SAGE and SimWorlds (planner, coder and reviewer agents with physics or deterministic verifiers), NuRec with Isaac Sim (splats in USD plus a simulator) and REALM (an MLLM agent that edits splats) (x3d_mcp; SAGE; SimWorlds; NuRec; REALM) SD. The proposal also defines what "robust, productive, optimal" can mean without overclaiming: optimal is a measured trade-off among gate pass rate, visual fidelity at a device splat budget, credits per accepted world, loop latency and critic agreement with humans, not a global optimum (Inference).

What AI-enabled X3D would look like

Picture an X3D 4.x scene whose splat worlds sit behind Inline nodes (GLB with KHR_gaussian_splatting, or SOG), whose Marble collider GLBs sit beside them as invisible shapes, and whose ROUTEs carry interactivity. Each subgraph has an owner agent with declared invariants recorded in node metadata; agents never write the file directly but submit patches to a transaction layer that validates, verifies, signs and commits them; and a planner turns goals into plans under a credit and rate budget. An illustrative loop (Speculation): a user asks for a mezzanine in a generated warehouse; the planner drafts a JSON plan; the splat curator requests a draft Marble world and runs the axis, scale and SH tests; the steward inserts an Inline and a Transform; the validator runs schema and splat-array rules; the physics checker drops test bodies on the collider; the verifier renders multi-view and normal-map views and consults a debiased VLM judge last; provenance signs the manifest; the commit lands or rolls back.

Four mechanisms make the agents "live inside" the graph rather than beside it.
